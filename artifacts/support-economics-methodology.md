# Support Economics Methodology

Support differs from a bounded project: it persists, demand is uncertain, interruptions arrive on someone else's schedule, and relationship work surrounds the technical work. A profitable project may therefore have an unprofitable tail. Recurring revenue is not automatically good revenue.

## Demand and work mix

Build a customer-level demand profile from requests, incidents, owner and partner effort, vendor coordination, training, configuration, documentation, after-hours events, recurrence, seasonality, and uncertainty. Preserve `UNKNOWN`; a short history is not an average. Ticket count is insufficient: ten five-minute questions and ten 90-minute vendor escalations impose different burdens.

Separate fixed costs (tools, account review, documentation upkeep, communication, partner readiness) from variable request, incident, partner, vendor, and after-hours costs. Separate owner work by triage, communication, diagnosis, coordination, QA/retest, education, administration, documentation, review, and interruption. Interruption risk is qualitative where precision is unsupported.

## Delivery dependencies

Record whether partner cost is hourly, prepaid, retained, per incident, warranty-included, vendor-included, or unknown. Cost does not prove availability: response reliability, after-hours coverage, continuity, and replacement difficulty constrain promises. Vendor fixes can be free while Local Works coordination remains costly.

## Plan design and fairness

Compare pay-as-you-go, prepaid hours, monthly fees, incident fees, and hybrids. Reuse the established support entitlement and state included request types, internal owner/partner capacity, incident treatment, vendor coordination, training, configuration, documentation, review cadence, business hours, and after-hours expectation. Exclude new workflows, integrations, redesign, migrations, large reports/training, unsupported systems, third-party fees, and discovery unless deliberately included.

Never sell warranty correction twice. Keep warranty and goodwill outside paid usage. Unlimited support lacks a natural cost ceiling when demand is uncertain and needs unusually strong evidence. Included hours are an internal capacity boundary, not the whole customer value proposition. State no, limited, or full rollover and explicit overage handling; never supply silent overage.

A fair plan does not rely on misunderstanding, fear, hidden exclusions, or seller delight at non-use. Customer value may include a known contact, quicker routing and recovery, reduced internal diagnosis, vendor coordination, continuity, and appropriate preventive work. Availability has insurance-like value, but fear is not evidence. Preventive documentation, configuration, dependency, workflow, QA, or account reviews belong only when useful. A lightweight review asks what happened, repeated, changed, and belongs in a project.

## Economics and capacity

The price floor covers expected partner and direct cost, administrative work, owner-time value, incident risk allowance, and reasonable contribution. Calculate both:

- contribution = revenue − partner cost − other direct cost;
- owner-time-adjusted contribution = contribution − imputed owner-time value.

Contribution margin is undefined at zero revenue. Contribution per owner hour exposes scarce-owner economics and should be compared with opportunity cost, while recognizing that family, education, sales, and focus are not always monetizable.

Break-even owner hours equal remaining contribution before owner time divided by the owner-hour value. Partner break-even uses the partner rate. Usage break-even depends on work mix, not ticket count. Avoid precision unsupported by assumptions.

Capacity starts with genuinely available owner hours, subtracts an incident reserve, then compares routine and stress demand. Utilization is a planning signal, not a universal target. Test concurrency, after-hours availability, and partner loss. Stress-test low/expected/high usage, routine and busy months, vendor and severe incidents, two incidents, after-hours events, partner unavailability, and doubled demand.

Simulate multiple months before annualizing revenue, direct cost, owner hours, contribution, adjusted contribution, and incidents. Review each customer before totals: revenue concentration can differ sharply from owner-hour or incident concentration. Rising goodwill can reveal an unpaid plan; rising warranty effort can reveal a quality problem; vendor burden can justify clearer limits, different delivery, vendor-led support, or declining support without blaming the customer.

## Verdicts

A verdict may be viable, viable with boundaries, higher price, lower scope, pay-as-you-go better, prepaid better, vendor support better, not economic, not operationally sustainable, or insufficient evidence. A hybrid may pair a base continuity fee with limited routine coordination and separately billed partner/project work. No recurring plan is a legitimate answer. This chapter records planning decisions only: it implements no billing, SLA, support portal, retention, or sales machinery.
