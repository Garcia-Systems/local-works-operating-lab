# Acquisition funnel hypotheses

> **HYPOTHETICAL TRAINING ASSUMPTIONS — NOT OBSERVED LOCAL WORKS DATA**
>
> **SIMULATED OUTPUT IS NOT OBSERVED EVIDENCE.** A simulated sale is neither an acquired customer nor proof of a conversion rate.

## 1. Funnel vocabulary

| Stage | Working definition |
|---|---|
| Exposure | Someone had an opportunity to encounter Local Works. |
| Website visit | Someone intentionally visited a Local Works page. |
| Audit start | Someone began the Digital Friction Audit. |
| Audit completion | Someone completed enough information for Local Works to review it. |
| Lead | A person or organization provided enough information or engagement for possible follow-up. |
| Qualified lead | A lead appears relevant enough to justify more Local Works attention. |
| Discovery | A meaningful discovery conversation/process occurs. |
| Proposal | Local Works presents a specific commercial recommendation. |
| Sale | The customer accepts an engagement under agreed commercial terms. |

A sale is not cash collection, completed delivery, or profit. Activity is not business progress: impressions, visits, starts, completions, opportunities, proposals, and customers are not interchangeable.

## 2–4. Baseline and conversion assumptions

The website/content training path begins with 10,000 hypothetical exposures.

| Transition | Rate | Evidence status |
|---|---:|---|
| Exposure → website visit | 2% | HYPOTHESIS |
| Website visit → audit start | 15% | HYPOTHESIS |
| Audit start → audit completion | 25% | HYPOTHESIS |
| Audit completion → qualified lead | 40% | HYPOTHESIS |
| Qualified lead → discovery | 60% | HYPOTHESIS |
| Discovery → proposal | 50% | HYPOTHESIS |
| Proposal → sale | 30% | HYPOTHESIS |

Expected arithmetic yields 200 visits, 30 starts, 7.5 completions, 3 qualified leads, 1.8 discoveries, 0.9 proposals, and **0.27 expected sales**. Fractions are planning values, not people. None is a forecast or recommendation.

## 5. Channel-specific differences

- **Website/content:** Exposure → Website Visit → Audit Start → Audit Completion → Qualified Lead → Discovery → Proposal → Sale.
- **Personalized outreach:** Target Identified → Outreach → Response → Qualified Conversation → Discovery → Proposal → Sale.
- **Referral:** Referral → Qualified Lead → Discovery → Proposal → Sale.

Each path has independently hypothetical rates. A referral begins deeper than an exposure, but this artifact makes no claim that referrals convert better. Paths need not pass through a website or online audit.

## Quality as well as volume

Scenario A could turn 100 leads into 10 qualified leads, 5 proposals, and 2 good customers; scenario B could turn 500 leads into 20 qualified leads, 8 proposals, and 1 bad-fit customer. This annotation is illustrative, not data. Local Works should seek economically sensible opportunities, not maximum lead count. A low qualification rate can be healthy filtering; **UNKNOWN is not BAD**, and drop-off is not automatically failure.

## 6. Owner-time assumptions

Every figure is a **HYPOTHESIS**. Website/content assigns 0.12 minutes per exposure (a notional 20 content hours), 12 minutes per completed audit review, 60 per discovery, and 90 per proposal. Outreach assigns 12 minutes per target, 8 per outreach, 15 per qualified conversation, 60 per discovery, and 90 per proposal. Referral assigns 10 minutes per referral, 15 per qualified lead, 60 per discovery, and 90 per proposal. These estimate acquisition hours only—not salary, CAC, delivery, or profitability.

## 7. Important unknowns

Actual entry volumes, buyer identity, attribution, conversion rates, lead quality, time per activity, channel cash costs, sales-cycle timing, and downstream economics are unknown. Whether the audit helps qualification and whether filtering protects capacity are also unknown.

## 8. Measurements needed from real experiments

Record source and entry stage; unique entrants and timestamps at each meaningful stage; explicit stage definitions; completions and losses; qualification reasons and unknowns; owner minutes; and outcomes linked to the originating cohort. Preserve observations separately from interpretations. Traffic alone cannot validate demand.

## 9. Revision conditions

Revise an assumption when consistently defined real cohorts produce measured rates, stage definitions prove ambiguous, attribution is unreliable, prospect quality changes, a channel uses a different path, owner-time logs differ materially, or qualitative replies show the modeled progression is wrong. Record method, cohort, period, and evidence provenance. Never promote a simulation result to observed or measured evidence.
