# Ideal Customer Hypothesis

> **INITIAL HYPOTHESIS — NOT VALIDATED**
>
> Local Works has no real customer evidence yet. This document is a prioritization hypothesis to test, not a declaration of a target market.

## 1. Initial ICP hypothesis

Promising Local Works customers may tend to have recurring customer or employee workflows, visible friction, meaningful manual work, and enough workflow volume for small inefficiencies to accumulate. They may also offer reachable decision makers, some ability to pay, technically solvable problems, manageable procurement, accessible sales channels, and outcomes that can be measured.

These traits justify investigation; they do not prove that an organization should purchase a project. Local Works is not searching for anyone who “needs software.” It is searching for situations where meaningful friction may create an economically sensible opportunity for improvement.

## 2. Positive indicators

- A customer or employee workflow happens daily, weekly, or many times per day.
- Manual entry, calls, spreadsheets, repeated email, paper re-entry, or disconnected systems consume meaningful effort.
- The effect is visible to staff or customers and may be tied to time, errors, delay, completion, retention, or another observable outcome.
- An owner or authorized manager is reachable.
- Purchasing and delivery constraints appear manageable.
- The organization plausibly has economic capacity, without relying on an invented revenue cutoff.
- Available technology and delivery partners could support a safe, proportionate response.
- A first engagement might naturally expose other real improvements; this is not permission to manufacture upsells.

## 3. Negative indicators

These lower priority or require investigation; none is automatically disqualifying:

- The workaround is essentially harmless or infrequent.
- Urgency, budget, authority, or impact is uncertain.
- Procurement, security, legal, or corporate approvals appear complex.
- Legacy, custom, corporate-controlled, or numerous disconnected systems increase delivery risk.
- The organization is difficult to reach through practical sales channels.
- The company is very small, has limited technology, already owns software, has a skeptical owner, or presents a simple problem.

The last group especially demands caution: small does not mean uneconomic, existing software may be configurable, and simple can be valuable. **UNKNOWN is different from BAD:** budget unknown is not no budget; authority unknown is not no authority; burden unknown is not low burden.

## 4. Hard disqualifiers

- The prospect expects illegal or unethical work.
- No plausible business problem exists.
- The prospect refuses necessary discovery while demanding a fixed solution.
- The work clearly cannot be delivered safely.
- The contact cannot authorize any purchase and cannot connect Local Works to someone who can.

A hard disqualifier supports a stop. A weak signal supports caution, proportionate inquiry, or lower priority.

## 5. Important unknowns

Before qualification, Local Works may need actual workflow frequency and volume, affected people, staff time, customer effects, workaround burden, urgency, economic impact, budget/capacity, approval authority, stakeholders, procurement constraints, system capabilities, vendor contracts, security/legal requirements, technical feasibility, useful baselines, and a practical route to the prospect.

Unknowns must remain explicit questions instead of being silently scored as failures.

## 6. Industries worth testing

Possible test markets include fitness and gyms, restaurants, studios/classes, local shops/services, recreation/attractions, membership organizations, home/trade services, professional services, and nonprofits/community organizations.

**These are test markets, not proven Local Works verticals.** Local Works claims no expertise or results in them.

## 7. Why industry alone is insufficient

Two gyms can have opposite fit. A fictional owner-controlled gym with recurring manual work and reachable authority may warrant an audit. A fictional corporate franchise location with the same member friction may have no local authority and an inaccessible procurement path. Organization-level workflow, economics, authority, complexity, feasibility, and access matter; **industry ≠ ICP**.

## 8. Evidence required to revise the ICP

Revise the hypothesis when real audits and discovery provide patterns in workflow volume, problem impact, willingness and ability to pay, access to authority, sales-channel response, procurement effort, technical feasibility, measurable improvement, delivery effort, and responsible follow-on work. Preserve counterexamples and declined/no-project outcomes rather than learning only from wins. Repeated evidence should outweigh anecdotes.

## 9. Current evidence level

The current evidence is conceptual analysis plus fictional exercises. There are no real Local Works customers, measured outcomes, conversion data, industry results, or validated verticals. Confidence is therefore low, and the proper next step is testing—not certainty.
