# Launch, commercial closeout, and economics methodology

Acceptance proves agreed criteria in the reviewed environment; it does not activate production. Record an accepted project as launch-pending until proportional **launch readiness** establishes access, authorization, an understood configuration/deployment method, responsible people, critical verification, vendor availability where relevant, and no blocking defect. A disclosed nonblocker may support `READY_WITH_MONITORED_RISKS`; it is not silently erased.

## Launch controls

Launch means enabling configuration for **CONFIGURE**, activating a connection for **INTEGRATE**, enabling a workflow for **AUTOMATE**, and deployment only for **CUSTOM BUILD**. Choose immediate, scheduled, phased, pilot, parallel, feature-toggle, manual, or not-applicable cutover in proportion to risk. Do not force release engineering onto a tiny SaaS setting. A launch plan names who authorizes, performs, verifies, communicates, and decides reversal; production authority may not remain ambiguous. Business, vendor, customer, and support-availability constraints determine the window—not a blanket assumption that nights are safer.

Before launch, state what changes, when, likely impact, and contact. Afterwards record completion, known issues, what to watch, and the handoff path; these are records, not real messages. Rollback can be available, partial, manual, unavailable, not applicable, or unknown. For configuration it may be restoring recorded settings; for integration it may disable the connection and restore the prior process. Data integrity, security/access, unusable core workflow, repeated wrong processing, or severe impact can justify pause/rollback; a cosmetic incident can justify continue-with-fix.

**Production verification** checks only critical behavior—workflow, status/data, notification, permissions, active dependency, duplicate/error absence, and staff operational step. It can fail despite staging acceptance and is not full QA. A short stabilization period records stable, monitoring, issues found, rollback required, or extended stabilization. Incident severity, evidence, response, and communication determine continue, fix, pause, rollback, partial disablement, or vendor escalation. Support execution begins in Chapter 27, not here.

## Commercial truth and payment

Delivery complete and commercial complete are separate. Commercial completion reconciles acceptance evidence, original charge, approved paid changes, credits/direct expenses, closeout documents, disputes, and the final obligation. Simulated payment statuses include not due, due, invoiced, received, partial, late, disputed, waived, and written off. Preserve due point, invoice date, due date, and received date. Total charges are original price plus approved paid changes less credits; final due is total charges less receipts. Revenue/charge is not cash. A scope dispute returns to immutable Chapter 24 evidence rather than guesswork or legal advice. Days outstanding, contractor cash already paid, and owner effort expose late-payment risk; no invoicing, money movement, or collections occurs here.

## Actual economics and learning

Project contribution is total customer charges less delivery-partner and other direct project costs. Contribution margin divides that by charges, but stays unknown/undefined at zero charges. Imputed owner-time value equals owner hours times the chosen internal value; contribution after owner time subtracts it **once**. Contribution per owner hour divides pre-owner-time contribution by owner hours and describes business yield, not wages or accounting profit.

Track acquisition, audit, discovery, design, proposal/sales, closing, coordination, requirements translation, project management, QA, change control, communication, and closeout. Show delivery-only and full acquisition-through-close views because unpaid presales is real burden. Preserve original estimate, revised forecast, and actual; cost, effort, and schedule variances teach only when history is not rewritten. Paid changes raise charges; absorbed changes, discovery overruns, defect correction, retest, and QA escapes may raise internal cost without customer price. A healthy headline margin can still hide excessive owner time.

Cash events form a timeline. The largest amount below zero in cumulative receipts less payments is maximum cash exposure. Thus a profitable project may be cash-negative while a partner has been paid and the customer receivable remains open.

## Customer and organizational learning

Expected recoverable value is a hypothesis, not measured value. Freeze the pre-project baseline and schedule relevant measures—such as interventions, minutes per request, contacts, exception rate, processing time, and rework—at 30/60/90 days. Preserve convenience, frustration reduction, clarity, and uncertainty reduction as non-monetized benefits rather than invented dollars.

Assess scope, quality, schedule, acceptance, collection, contribution, owner time, customer-value plausibility, partner performance, continuity, and relationship independently as strong, acceptable, mixed, weak, or unknown. No magic score can turn technical success into economic success, or margin into customer value. A blame-free postmortem asks what estimates, assumptions, wording, rework, partner behavior, and owner activities mattered and what to repeat.

Partner evidence—estimate accuracy, communication, quality, documentation, reliability, defect response, handoff, and judgment—may guide future private selection, never a fake public rating. Reuse sanitized questions, workflow/requirements patterns, tests, checklists, and control lessons; never customer data, credentials, proprietary code, or confidential rules. A public case study remains measurement- and permission-dependent, with accurate attribution and confidentiality review; do not invent a testimonial.

Closeout reviews launch/stabilization, acceptance, defects/known issues, documentation, configuration/source control, partner and customer payments, economics, measurement, lessons, and the warranty/support boundary. Temporary access is recorded as retained, reduced, revoked, transferred, or reviewed without modifying real access. Handoff identifies known issues, documents, contact path, vendor dependencies, warranty assumptions, and operational ownership. Operational closure may coexist with measurement pending, known issues, or outstanding payment; disputes and incomplete stabilization remain explicit.
