# Delivery-path methodology

## Principle and three-party model

**The delivery partner is not the product.** The customer owns its business problem and decisions. Local Works owns the customer relationship, problem and scope context, solution leadership, translation, project coordination, commercial accountability, documentation continuity, QA coordination, and support coordination. A delivery team supplies some or all implementation capability. Local Works is neither merely a developer reseller nor obliged to make one developer do everything; useful direct technical conversation can occur without surrendering relationship leadership.

## Requirements before candidates

Derive required and optional capabilities from the selected solution and bounded scope before looking at names. Preserve the evidence and importance of configuration, integration, automation, development, data, deployment, security, testing, design, accessibility, documentation, handoff, and scale needs. Unknowns remain unknown. A good technician can still be the wrong fit.

## Paths and solution matching

Consider self-delivery, independent or specialist freelancers, small or larger agencies, platform partners, vendor professional services, customer internal teams, mixed teams, and no external delivery. None is inherently best. Configure may fit Local Works, a platform specialist, or vendor services; integrate and automate may need specialists; a custom build may warrant a developer or agency; leave-alone needs no provider. Never use a custom-build provider's preference to force complexity.

Self-delivery offers context and control but creates capacity, skill, distraction, support, and key-person risks. Contractors offer flexibility and specialization but may have availability, capacity, backup, documentation, and continuity risks. Agencies provide a broader team and process but may cost more, add communication layers, or prefer larger work. Vendor services bring platform knowledge but may constrain customization or increase dependency. Customer teams retain internal knowledge but face priorities and skill gaps. Mixed teams combine strengths while increasing coordination and ownership ambiguity.

## Fit and evidence

Assess technical and platform fit, integration experience, security awareness, testing, documentation, communication, availability, capacity, reliability, support handoff, and cost fit with explicit `STRONG`, `ADEQUATE`, `UNCERTAIN`, `WEAK`, or `NO_FIT` judgments—not a magic score. Evidence provenance matters: self-report, public portfolio, reference, direct evaluation, past performance, certification, and unknown are not interchangeable. Never invent background checks or Local Works history.

Communication evidence concerns predictable response, useful questions, clear tradeoffs, surfaced uncertainty, documentation, and escalation—not geography, nationality, accent, or superficial fluency. Reliability concerns commitments, early warning, realistic estimates, meetings, and recorded decisions. Availability and capacity are separate: five available hours do not meet a twenty-hour weekly need.

## Cost and total risk

Cost is one dimension. Low cost can carry rework and continuity exposure; higher cost can carry process and handoff benefits. Neither cheapest nor most expensive wins automatically. Examine skill, capacity, communication, reliability, quality, security, source and credential control, subcontracting, support, lock-in, customer dependency, and key-person risk alongside cost.

## Continuity controls

Ask what remains if the provider disappears tomorrow. Requirements, acceptance criteria, decisions, issue history, source, architecture and deployment notes, test evidence, account administration, and appropriate credential control must remain transitionable. For custom work, prefer a customer- and/or Local Works-controlled repository as agreed—not a contractor's personal account as the sole copy.

Record each third-party account's **account owner, administrative access, and recovery path**. Do not place needed services only under a contractor's private identity. Never email passwords casually: prefer customer-controlled accounts, separate least-privilege users, temporary access, revocation, and project-appropriate secret handling. This is operational guidance, not a legal ownership rule or formal security certification.

Scale setup, configuration, integration, deployment, limitation, troubleshooting, and handoff documentation to project complexity. “The developer says it works” is not acceptance: assess reproducibility, environments, defect correction, test practice, and acceptance support. For sensitive work, inspect minimized access, data handling, credential practice, logging, dependencies, vulnerability awareness, and escalation.

## Subcontracting, incentives, and backup

Subcontracting may be none, possible, expected, or unknown. Make material use visible: who works, who gains access, who communicates, who remains accountable, and whether notice or approval is required. It is not inherently bad.

A provider paid more for custom development has an incentive that Local Works must separate from customer strategy. Preserve the configure → integrate → automate → custom build → leave alone hierarchy independently.

Solo providers are not automatically excluded, but one person holding code, deployment, credentials, architecture, and vendor knowledge creates key-person risk. Mitigate with shared repositories and accounts, incremental documentation and handoffs, Local Works knowledge retention, and a transition-ready backup path. A backup need not mean paying two teams.

## Sourcing and prequalification

Possible channels include professional networks, previous collaborators, referrals, specialist agencies, vendor directories, freelance marketplaces, professional communities, LinkedIn, local technical networks, customer recommendations, and development companies. Sourcing is not outreach or evidence that any real provider is available.

Before detailed estimates, confirm relevant work and stack, project size, availability **and** capacity, willingness to work with Local Works as customer-facing lead, communication, documentation, handoff, security/access, repository/account, subcontracting, and commercial expectations. Undocumented systems, uncertain APIs, difficult migrations, or unavailable vendor access may responsibly require paid technical discovery; estimates need not always be free.

Only `QUALIFIED_FOR_ESTIMATE` candidates pass to Chapter 19. Other honest results are `NEEDS_MORE_INFORMATION`, `NOT_QUALIFIED`, `NOT_AVAILABLE`, `WRONG_DELIVERY_MODEL`, and `TOO_RISKY`. This gate neither obtains an estimate nor chooses a winner.

## Local Works must be a good client

Local Works provides clear scope, answers questions, consolidates feedback, makes timely decisions, pays agreed terms, controls scope, prevents contradictory instructions, and does not demand indefinite unpaid exploration. Durable delivery networks are reciprocal professional relationships.
