# Digital Friction Audit Methodology

## Purpose

The audit asks whether there is enough evidence of meaningful customer or employee
workflow friction to justify further investigation. It is an entry diagnostic, not
a disguised software proposal. It can document a workflow, establish bounded facts,
identify questions, and recommend whether to stop, monitor, make an obvious simple
improvement, or conduct discovery.

It cannot establish unmeasured frequency, financial impact, user sentiment, root
cause, technical feasibility, or an implementation choice. It cannot guarantee a
project and never recommends a custom build.

## Two connected views

Map only the relevant stages from **Find → Understand → Contact → Book or Join →
Pay → Receive Service → Manage → Return**. Alongside the customer path, follow the
employee/operational path: who receives information, re-enters it, changes systems,
waits, approves exceptions, communicates status, and reconciles results. A smooth
public form can conceal substantial internal handling; conversely, a friendly call
may be valuable service.

## Observation is not inference

Write an **observed fact** that the cited source supports. Put the possible
consequence in a separate **friction hypothesis**. “The site requires a telephone
request” does not establish that customers dislike calls, that staff burden is high,
that money is being lost, or that a portal would help.

Evidence sources include public websites, public booking or membership flows,
public documents, customer/employee/manager statements, direct observation, system
demonstrations, process documentation, and measured data. A public observation is
often sufficient to form a question. Statements can corroborate context. Direct
workflow observation and measured data can more strongly support process, frequency,
or burden claims. Source labels do not eliminate the need to inspect method and scope.

## Significance without a magic score

For each finding, expose frequency, severity, affected parties, manual effort,
customer inconvenience, error potential, business criticality, evidence strength,
and the reasoning that connects them. Do not collapse these into unexplained points.
`UNKNOWN` means not established; it is not a synonym for low. Working processes and
low-significance observations belong in the report so the audit does not manufacture
a sales opportunity.

## Human service and competing objectives

A phone call, email, paper step, or in-person exchange is not inherently friction.
Ask whether the workflow requires it unnecessarily and whether the interaction adds
human value. Record customer friction, the business objective, the policy reason,
any contractual or regulatory reason, and the tradeoff. Neither convenience nor a
retention preference automatically wins.

## Recommendation categories

- **No meaningful friction:** current evidence offers no reason to proceed.
- **Monitor:** retain a plausible observation and watch for stronger evidence.
- **Simple improvement:** a bounded, low-risk operational correction is apparent;
  this is not a software architecture decision.
- **Discovery recommended:** evidence supports focused internal investigation.
- **Insufficient information:** do not infer a problem; gather only proportionate
  evidence if doing so is worthwhile.

Discovery is warranted when evidence indicates a repeated or consequential workflow
issue, multiple parties or a critical process may be affected, and important internal
facts can be learned through a bounded follow-up. Discovery recommended does not mean
project recommended.

## Ethical and public-observation boundaries

Use only information intentionally public and normal public flows; identify the
fictional or simulated status of training material. Do not scrape, automate, evade
controls, submit disruptive forms, impersonate customers, access private systems, or
publish speculative claims as facts. Obtain permission before internal observation,
minimize personal data, and report limitations. Stop when evidence indicates an
economically harmless process or a justified exception.
