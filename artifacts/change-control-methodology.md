# Change Control Methodology

## Purpose and fairness
Change control protects the customer from surprise charges and Local Works from unlimited unpriced work. Its purpose is a fair boundary—not maximum change-order revenue. Begin with new information, classify it, compare it with the approved baseline, analyze incremental impact, offer alternatives, record authority and decision, then update baseline and forecast only when warranted.

## Classify before discussing money
A **clarification** adds non-material detail already reasonably implied. A **defect** fails an approved requirement or acceptance criterion. A **delivery correction** remedies an agreed technical responsibility (configuration, mapping, tests, or documentation). These are generally not charged. A **requirement correction** repairs misunderstood business meaning; investigate what was known, unknowable, supplied incorrectly, or missed without reflexive blame. **Technical discovery** is new feasibility evidence. A **dependency change** records the dependency, change, controller, impact, and response options. Neither is automatically customer creep. A **customer enhancement** adds desired capability; a material change to workflow, actors, systems, locations, data, integration, reporting, acceptance, timeline, responsibility, or support is a true **scope change**. A **deferred idea** survives for later without entering delivery. Use **UNKNOWN**, request clarification, investigate, or review scope history rather than guess.

## Compare the baseline
Compare the request with immutable approved scope and requirement versions, acceptance, proposal, decisions, statements, assumptions, and estimate. Record included, excluded, ambiguous, or unknown—not merely feature count. Poor wording such as “support membership changes” creates **scope ambiguity** and commercial risk; it is not proof of customer fault.

## Materiality and impact
Assess TRIVIAL, SMALL, MATERIAL, MAJOR, or UNKNOWN from effort, cost, schedule, risk, complexity, dependencies, systems, process, testing, and documentation; there is no magic threshold. Record additional delivery, Local Works, and customer hours with confidence and assumptions. Analyze scope, quality, testing, documentation, dependencies, customer effort, owner time, and partner time. Preserve baseline forecast, impact, and revised forecast rather than overwriting history.

Delivery cost and customer price are separate decisions. A cost can be absorbed, charged, traded, or deferred. For a paid material change compare incremental customer value, price, delivery cost, owner time, contribution, schedule, and risk using Chapter 15 logic—not contractor cost plus a fixed markup and not a fictional recomputation of the whole project. A $500/year benefit does not justify an $8,000 addition merely because it was requested.

## Options and authority
Options include ABSORB, APPROVE_WITHOUT_PRICE_CHANGE, APPROVE_WITH_PRICE_CHANGE, TRADE_SCOPE, PHASE_LATER, DEFER, REJECT, RETURN_FOR_CLARIFICATION, REVISIT_SOLUTION, REVISIT_SCOPE, and PAUSE_PROJECT. A scope trade explicitly removes equivalent work and changes the baseline by agreement. Phasing preserves value and launch; rejection is appropriate when unsafe, infeasible, irrational, conflicting, unfunded, or a poor fit.

Customer decision makers approve customer commitments; Local Works controls commercial offers and delivery responsibility; material scope, price, or schedule commitments are shared as applicable. A delivery partner alone cannot authorize commercial change. Status moves through submitted, classifying, impact analysis, awaiting decisions, approved/rejected/deferred/absorbed, withdrawn, or superseded.

## Decisions, baselines, and cumulative goodwill
For every material item preserve request and provenance, classification, baseline link, rationale, impact, options, approver, commercial and schedule treatment, and successor baseline. Approval creates a new scope or requirements version and acceptance/estimate/forecast linkage; it never edits v1 silently. Deferral does not change active scope.

Absorb a tiny change deliberately for negligible burden and goodwill when it poses no schedule or precedent risk—but record why and accumulate delivery and owner effort. Eight 30-minute favors are four hours; later requests may require review. Goodwill without boundaries becomes unlimited scope.

## Disputes and overruns
For “defect versus extra,” inspect requirements, scope, acceptance, and decision history instead of choosing customer or partner by assertion. A partner’s 20-hour overrun may reflect changed scope, invalidated assumptions, reasonable discovery, incorrect customer information, or poor estimation. Establish causality first. If a partner proposes rebuilding a portal, demand technical evidence and compare alternatives before passing cost to the customer.
