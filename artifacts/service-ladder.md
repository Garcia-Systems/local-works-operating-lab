# Local Works Service Ladder

This is the working customer-service model for Local Works. It answers **what Local Works is doing now**; it is separate from the Configure → Integrate → Automate → Custom Build → Leave Alone solution choices. Pricing and packaging remain undecided.

## 1. Digital Friction Audit

- **Purpose:** Identify meaningful friction in customer or employee workflows.
- **Customer question:** “Is there meaningful friction worth investigating?”
- **Local Works responsibility:** Listen, inspect the initial workflow context, distinguish symptoms from requested solutions, and recommend a proportionate next step.
- **Typical inputs:** Customer-reported concern, people involved, and initial workflow context.
- **Typical outputs:** Friction observations and a recommended next step.
- **Possible exit:** No meaningful issue; issue not worth solving; obvious configuration or process fix; or discovery recommended.
- **Does not guarantee:** Discovery, implementation, or a software project.

## 2. Discovery

- **Purpose:** Understand the business problem deeply enough to decide whether an economically sensible response exists.
- **Customer question:** “What is happening, why does it matter, and is it worth addressing?”
- **Local Works responsibility:** Reconstruct workflows; interview stakeholders; inventory systems; examine frequency, volume, burden, authority, urgency, constraints, and feasibility questions.
- **Typical inputs:** Audit findings, stakeholder access, workflow evidence, system context, and known constraints.
- **Typical outputs:** Validated problem statement, relevant economics and constraints, unresolved questions, and a decision brief.
- **Possible exit:** No project; gather more evidence; or proceed to solution design.
- **Does not guarantee:** That the problem deserves action or that Local Works will propose implementation.

## 3. Solution Design

- **Purpose:** Determine the simplest sensible response.
- **Customer question:** “Given the validated problem, what should we do?”
- **Local Works responsibility:** Compare leaving it alone, changing the process, configuring an existing system, integrating, automating, custom building, or combining approaches.
- **Typical inputs:** Validated problem, economics, constraints, systems inventory, and decision criteria.
- **Typical outputs:** Recommendation, alternatives and trade-offs, and—when appropriate—a delivery outline.
- **Possible exit:** Leave alone; make a process change; use a configuration fix; approve a response; or conclude no feasible response exists.
- **Does not guarantee:** Custom software, an implementation engagement, or any particular commercial packaging.

## 4. Implementation / Delivery

- **Purpose:** Execute the approved solution.
- **Customer question:** “How will the approved change be delivered safely and successfully?”
- **Local Works responsibility:** Provide appropriate project leadership, customer communication, coordination, and QA, even when a contractor, specialist, agency, SaaS vendor, internal team, or combination implements the work.
- **Typical inputs:** Approved solution, scope, acceptance criteria, constraints, and delivery resources.
- **Typical outputs:** Delivered change, quality evidence, documentation, launch, and operational handoff.
- **Possible exit:** Accepted delivery, handoff to operations, or a documented stop decision.
- **Does not guarantee:** That Local Works personally performs every technical task or that every engagement reaches launch regardless of new evidence.

## 5. Support

- **Purpose:** Help the customer operate the delivered solution after launch.
- **Customer question:** “How do we keep the delivered solution working in normal operation?”
- **Local Works responsibility:** Coordinate bug triage, configuration help, third-party issues, documentation, operational questions, minor changes, and incidents within agreed boundaries.
- **Typical inputs:** Supported solution, reported issue or question, operating context, and support boundaries.
- **Typical outputs:** Triage, guidance, coordination, documentation, resolution, or appropriate referral.
- **Possible exit:** Issue resolved; warranty work identified; request classified as a new project or feature expansion; or support relationship ends.
- **Does not guarantee:** Unlimited changes. Support, warranty obligations, new projects, and feature expansion are distinct.

## 6. Continuous Improvement / Account Development

- **Purpose:** Periodically identify additional worthwhile improvements without aggressive upselling.
- **Customer question:** “Has new friction emerged that is valuable enough to examine?”
- **Local Works responsibility:** Review operating experience, identify possible workflow, integration, reporting, automation, or operational improvements, and apply the same value and economics tests again.
- **Typical inputs:** Operating experience, new friction, customer priorities, and previous decisions.
- **Typical outputs:** Improvement observations, value assessment, and a recommended next step.
- **Possible exit:** No worthwhile change; revisit later; or begin a new audit or discovery effort.
- **Does not guarantee:** Recurring implementation, automatic expansion, or that every idea becomes an opportunity.

## Working principle

Local Works sells a disciplined path from **Problem → Understanding → Decision → Implementation → Ongoing operation**. A customer may receive real value from understanding a problem, changing a process, configuring current software, or deciding not to act. Custom implementation is only one possible outcome.
