# Pricing Methodology

## Purpose and separations

Price is a two-sided decision, not cost plus markup and not maximum value extraction. Keep **current customer burden**, **recoverable customer value**, **customer price**, **delivery cost**, **other direct project cost**, **owner effort**, **contribution**, and **customer return** as separate records. A delivery estimate predicts internal delivery cost; it is not the customer price or a quote. Customer price reflects bounded scope, responsibility, outcome, service, risk, and customer economics. Internal contractor cost need not be used as customer-facing justification.

Direct project cost includes delivery partners, project software/tools, contractors, specialists, travel, and other attributable expense. Do not silently load general business overhead into that category. Owner time—discovery follow-up, clarification, proposal preparation, coordination, meetings, translation, QA, project management, acceptance, issues, and administration—has an internal planning value even when no customer line item exposes it.

## Core calculations

- **Contribution** = customer price − delivery partner cost − other direct project costs.
- **Contribution margin** = contribution ÷ customer price when price is positive.
- **Contribution after owner-time value** additionally subtracts owner hours × internal owner-hour value.
- **Customer annual net benefit** = recoverable annual value − recurring solution cost (and other recurring operating burden when applicable).
- **Payback months** = candidate customer price (plus relevant one-time customer cost) ÷ annual net benefit × 12, only with positive supported benefit.
- **Multi-year customer result** = years × annual net benefit − price − other one-time customer cost.

Contribution is not accounting profit. Overhead, acquisition, taxes, insurance, and other expenses may remain. UNKNOWN cost or value should not become zero.

## Floor, ceiling, and window

A transparent Local Works floor can add expected direct delivery cost, other direct cost, imputed owner-time value, and an explicitly chosen required contribution. That contribution is a decision input—not a universal margin standard. Merely covering the contractor invoice is insufficient.

A customer ceiling may be derived from a customer-specific payback/downside guardrail, confidence, alternatives, internal burden, time to benefit, and ongoing costs. Recoverable value is not an automatic price. The overlap between a supported Local Works floor and supported customer ceiling is the **practical pricing window**. Missing evidence calls for better estimates or value evidence. Floor above ceiling means **NO HEALTHY PRICE**; simplify, reduce scope, change delivery, phase, support internal implementation, leave alone, or decline. This is a successful analytical result.

## Deal structures

- **Fixed fee:** useful when scope, dependencies, acceptance, and uncertainty are bounded. It gives certainty and rewards efficiency but exposes Local Works to creep, hidden dependencies, rework, and underestimation.
- **Time and materials:** useful during investigation or evolving requirements. Still state an expected range, budget guardrail, reporting cadence, and approval boundary.
- **Phased:** a paid validation/discovery phase can reduce both parties' risk before optional implementation. Each phase has a price and decision boundary.
- **Retainer:** recurring access to defined capacity or services.
- **Subscription:** an ongoing defined service. Never manufacture recurrence without recurring service/value.
- **Hybrid:** for example, paid discovery plus fixed implementation, or fixed implementation plus genuinely recurring support. Support pricing is deferred.

Not every model suits every engagement. Estimation uncertainty should prompt validation, narrower scope, phases, T&M, or explicit contingency—not disappear inside an unexplained percentage buffer.

## Payment, exposure, and discounts

Deposits demonstrate commitment, reserve capacity, fund startup, and reduce cash exposure, but are not automatically accounting revenue. Upfront, deposit/final, milestone, monthly, and completion structures distribute risk differently. Model events in time and find the largest cumulative deficit after customer receipts and direct payments: **maximum Local Works cash exposure**. Positive contribution does not prevent a cash crisis when suppliers are paid before the customer.

A discount comes entirely from contribution while direct costs stay fixed. At $6,000 price and $3,000 direct cost, a 10% discount creates a $5,400 price and $2,400 contribution: contribution falls 20%. Show this sensitivity before agreeing. Reducing price without changing scope is price negotiation; removing or changing work is scope negotiation. Other responses include changing implementation, phasing, timing, or declining.

## Failure modes and healthy-deal test

Blind cost-plus ignores owner time, scope, acquisition effort, risk, support exposure, and customer economics. Blind “percentage of value” ignores recoverability, evidence, alternatives, customer risk, internal effort, and time to benefit.

A healthy deal generally leaves the customer meaningful expected benefit, acceptable payback, manageable downside, reasonable confidence, and a sensible alternative comparison. It covers Local Works' direct costs, recognizes owner effort, leaves positive contribution, and keeps risk, cash exposure, and responsibility manageable. Failure on either side means restructure or no deal—not a forced sale. Pricing analysis does not issue a proposal or guarantee acceptance.
