# Support boundary methodology

## Purpose
Fair continuity avoids both abandonment and an unpriced, permanent support job. **Warranty** is the limited operational obligation to correct delivered work that fails approved scope, requirements, or acceptance criteria under the modeled assumptions. **Support** is post-launch help operating, maintaining, troubleshooting, coordinating, or adjusting it. These are operating concepts, not legal terms.

## Triage before treatment
Acknowledge, then ask only useful questions: what happened and when; who and what is affected; whether use continues; expected versus actual behavior; approved scope and prior operation; recent customer, environment, or vendor changes; workaround; and data/security implications. Preserve `UNKNOWN` when facts are insufficient. Priority follows impact, affected users, workaround, security/data, and time sensitivity—not punctuation in a subject line.

Classification separates defect/warranty candidate, how-to, documentation, configuration, routine support, training, incident, data/access, customer environment, third party, enhancement, new project, and unknown. It must precede commercial treatment. A ten-minute new capability remains new work; a three-hour investigation can remain warranty.

## Warranty assessment and timing
Link the request to requirements, acceptance criteria, delivered and expected behavior, acceptance history, intended use, customer/vendor/environment changes, and evidence. Outcomes are applies, likely, does not apply, more evidence required, disputed, or not applicable. A configurable hypothetical period (for example 30 days) is one fact, not an automatic moral or responsibility decision; an apparent latent defect deserves review after expiration.

A post-launch failure of an accepted requirement with no customer or external change is likely warranty and should not be automatically charged. A verified administrator rule change is instead a customer-environment issue and possibly configuration assistance—but never blame the customer without evidence. A vendor behavior change remains a third-party issue; Local Works can coordinate without pretending to control the platform.

## Support and new work
How-to and documentation questions may be answered from the runbook and should improve it when the answer ought to exist. Training for new staff depends on onboarding/support entitlement and is not a defect. Configuration assistance changes an existing non-policy setting; adding a business rule or capability is an enhancement. Materially different workflows, systems, locations, reporting suites, or platforms return to discovery, economics, and design as a new project; do not quote before sufficient evidence.

An incident is an unexpected interruption or degradation needing coordinated response. Security, suspicious access, exposure, credentials, or apparent data loss receive high-priority incident routing. Chapter 27 classifies and routes only; it does not perform incident response.

## Entitlement, responsibility, and money
Record whether entitlement is none, warranty-only, limited, monthly, prepaid, incident-only, or custom. A support plan may record included types/capacity, response expectation, exclusions, third-party coordination, after-hours assumptions, term, and simulated status. It is not an invented SLA or contract.

Responsibility may be Local Works, customer, partner, vendor, shared, or undetermined. Confirm partner defect response, charges, handoff, and contact ownership rather than assuming free partner support. Escalation may be customer → Local Works → partner → vendor, but direct vendor contact can be better. Own customer communication under the chosen relationship model without becoming an unnecessary middleman.

Commercial treatments include no-charge warranty, included, prepaid or billable support, quote, new-project discovery, customer/vendor handles, intentional goodwill, and decline. Customers without a plan still receive enough triage to identify a fair path—not silence or unlimited work. Explain classifications helpfully rather than saying only “not covered.” Response means acknowledgment and triage; resolution means the outcome is fixed, so a fast response is not a same-day resolution promise.

## Learning and real effort
Track triage, communication, diagnosis, partner/vendor coordination, resolution, and documentation owner time—even a quick email. Track goodwill count, owner/partner time, and internal cost so relationship gestures do not become invisible recurring work. Recurring questions can indicate weak usability, documentation, training, a defect, a missing feature, or process mismatch and should trigger review rather than endless answers.

Support history is discovery evidence for needs, assumptions, and failure patterns. An enhancement can become an opportunity link without turning every problem into an upsell. When accounts conflict, preserve `DISPUTED` or `MORE_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED` while collecting timeline, requirements, change history, and conceptual logs/reports.
