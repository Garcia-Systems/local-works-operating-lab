# Commercial closing methodology

## The control boundary

Accepted in principle is a useful sales outcome, not permission to incur cost or begin delivery. Keep interest, verbal yes, proposal acceptance, agreement readiness/execution, invoice/payment, preconditions, authorization, and implementation as separate facts. Closing protects the customer from ambiguous scope and Local Works from accidental financing or commitments.

## Establish commercial truth and authority

Freeze a reference to the accepted proposal, scope, pricing, payment structure, assumptions, and exclusions. “The proposal” is inadequate when several versions exist. Reconcile any version or scope disagreement before authorization. Record display names separately from contracting entities; do not invent Harbor Fitness's legal entity. Local Works is the customer-facing initiative, while Garcia Systems is the operating-company context, subject to appropriate confirmation.

Identify the decision maker, budget owner, signer, technical approver, and procurement contact where relevant. A supporter need not have binding authority. Required unconfirmed or insufficient authority is a blocker.

## Make the agreement ready

A proposal is a commercial decision document. An agreement establishes the understood working relationship. A signed proposal may be part of an agreement in practice, but qualified counsel determines legal sufficiency. Before execution, review entity names, versioned scope, price/payment, responsibilities, assumptions, exclusions, acceptance, change handling, cancellation, ownership/licensing, third parties, support/warranty, confidentiality, and risk allocation. This methodology provides no jurisdiction-specific clauses.

## Protect payment and commitments

Represent invoice/payment state; do not process money. If a deposit is required, an executed agreement alone is not authorization. Record every potential commitment—including reservation, setup fee, license, travel, hardware, vendor charge, and retainer—with amount, payee, purpose, refundability, customer funds, approver, and status. **No informal commitments.**

Cash coverage equals customer funds received minus immediate commitments. A deficit is near-term cash exposure even if later project contribution is positive. Paying a $3,000 vendor first on a reimbursement promise requires review of responsibility, timing, refundability, commitment, and funding—not an automatic yes.

## Stage the unknowns

Classify preconditions as before authorization, implementation, or launch. Signer confirmation can block authorization; test access may block implementation; launch policy can wait until launch. Preserve critical assumptions from discovery, solution, scope, pricing, and proposal. An unresolved capability or API assumption may justify a paid validation phase or technical hold rather than pretending full implementation is ready.

Use a transparent checklist whose entries are satisfied, not satisfied, not applicable, or unknown. No score replaces judgment. Hard blockers include unclear versions/scope, authority, missing required agreement/deposit, material scope disagreement, invalidated assumptions, unacceptable exposure, unavailable required access, or unavailable delivery path. Soft risks—review availability, minor documentation gaps, optional details, low-impact schedule uncertainty—remain visible without making every unknown fatal.

## Decide deliberately

The available outcomes are authorize the next stage, hold for requirement/payment/validation/customer action, restructure, decline, or cancel before start. Authorization permits delivery preparation; it does not select a delivery partner, commit a start date, or begin implementation.

If a customer cancels before start, record work performed, incurred costs, refundable and non-refundable commitments, and whether payment may be owed under agreed terms. If cancellation follows signing/payment, do not improvise: review the governing agreement, reservations, costs, cancellation terms, and communication. **Commercial treatment depends on the governing agreement; seek qualified advice.**

## Questions that must not disappear

Maintain an ownership register for custom deliverables, customer data, reusable methods, third-party software, licenses, repository/access control, and partner continuity. Request minimum necessary, revocable access; prefer test data; do not casually share credentials or allow customer data to become contractor property. Surface whether subcontractors are allowed, require notice/approval, or remain unknown. External products have independent price, terms, availability, limits, support, and policy changes that Local Works cannot permanently guarantee.

Clarify that out-of-scope changes require evaluation, impact analysis, customer decision, and possible price/timeline revision. Carry objective Chapter 14 acceptance criteria into the close. Bound post-launch defect correction, separate support, new features, and third-party problems without designing Chapter 27 support.

Finally, separate a target start from a committed start. When capacity or the delivery partner is unknown, only a tentative target is honest. Chapter 18—not this close—finds the delivery path.
