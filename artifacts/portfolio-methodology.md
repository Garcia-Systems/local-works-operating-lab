# Local Works portfolio methodology

All examples are fictional. This methodology combines Chapter 31A's weekly portfolio foundation with Chapter 31B's through-time test; it is neither a CRM nor accounting software.

## Foundation retained from 31A

Use one `CustomerPortfolio`, its `PortfolioCustomer` references, unified `PortfolioWorkItem` inventory, finite `OwnerCapacity`, `DeliveryCapacity`, and `SupportCapacity`, explicit conflicts, risks, concentration, and gated starts. Urgency follows business impact, commitments, blocking dependencies, and cash risk—not customer size. Keep pipeline potential separate from booked revenue.

## Cash flow, receivables, and partner timing

For every period, begin with configurable finite cash and sequence simulated customer receipts and partner/direct/overhead payments by day. Track receivables as not due, due, late, partially received, received, disputed, or simulated write-off. Customer revenue may be recognized while its cash remains receivable. Partner deposits, milestones, finals, and support cost may occur before customer receipts. The lowest timed balance is the minimum cash position. **Maximum cash exposure** is the greatest cumulative amount Local Works must fund before corresponding cash arrives, also considering uncovered future commitments. Profit does not equal liquidity.

## Revenue and contribution

Report project, support, expansion, and other revenue separately; never include pipeline. Report their contributions separately from incident/warranty burden and other direct costs. Neither revenue nor contribution is cash. Owner sales, audit, qualification, discovery, design, proposal, coordination, QA, support, incident, relationship, collection, administration, and context-switch hours remain visible; contribution per owner hour is descriptive, not a magic score.

## Multi-month simulation

Advance twelve deterministic monthly periods through lead, qualification, discovery, proposal, sale, queue, authorized start, active delivery, completion, and support. Aggregate only the detail needed to see lifecycle movement. Each completion adds a recurring support tail. Incidents use reserve; correlated vendor incidents can affect multiple otherwise diversified customers. A primary-partner availability reduction blocks starts and expands the visible queue.

Compare opportunity volume with expected delivery slots to label pipeline weak, adequate, strong, or excessive for capacity. A start moves from signed to queued, scheduled, authorized, then active only after owner hours, partner hours, cash exposure, support reserve, and existing commitments pass. Deferred work and commitments affected by overload remain explicit.

## Capacity through time

For every month show available, planned, unplanned incident, context-switch, and remaining hours. Required hours above sustainable capacity are overload. One isolated month is temporary stretch; repeated overload is chronic. Delivery can displace selling, causing a delayed pipeline cliff. Completed projects can individually look harmless while their cumulative support tail displaces sales. High demand can increase both revenue and operational failure.

## Concentration

Track the largest customer's shares of revenue, contribution, owner hours, support burden, and receivables separately. Track primary delivery, specialist, support-partner, and shared-vendor dependence. The measures need not identify the same concentration. Rising concentration is a risk flag, not an automatic rejection.

## Decisions and scenarios

Run baseline, conservative, growth, and plausible stress scenarios. Test a promising marginal deal against portfolio capacity, a profitable deal against available cash, a high-support deal against reserve, and a largest-customer expansion against concentration. Permitted responses include authorize, queue, defer, re-time payment conceptually, set support boundaries, refer out, or decline for now. Good deal does not mean good deal right now.

Evaluate pipeline, sales, delivery, support, incidents, cash, customer concentration, partner resilience, vendor risk, owner capacity, quality, and relationships separately as strong, acceptable, mixed, weak, or unknown. Then issue an evidence-backed primary portfolio verdict plus qualifiers; do not manufacture a composite score.

## Operating reviews

The weekly review controls immediate collisions and reserve. The monthly business review persists customer movement, funnel, delivery, support, financial timing, owner hours, concentration, risks, decisions, and next-month actions. Period snapshots make trends reviewable.

## Boundary

Chapter 31's verdict is about portfolio coordination. It does not calculate final owner income, run a 36-month or Monte Carlo final examination, hire, process payroll/tax, invoice, keep books, or decide final business viability. Those questions remain outside Chapter 31.
