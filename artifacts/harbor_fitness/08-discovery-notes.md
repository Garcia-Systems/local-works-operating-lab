# Harbor Fitness — Chapter 8 discovery notes

> **FICTIONAL TRAINING SCENARIO — NOT A REAL CUSTOMER INTERVIEW**

## Opportunity hypothesis entering discovery
“Routine membership-account creation and changes appear to require staff intervention and, in at least the demonstrated join path, entry into a separate system. This may create member inconvenience and repetitive administrative work.” This is Chapter 7's current problem hypothesis, not a validated problem.

## Participants
- Fictional owner/general manager
- Fictional front-desk employee

No member was interviewed. The membership-manager perspective remains absent.

## Questions asked
Both interviews explored the current freeze process, frequency, roles, normal and exception handling time, exceptions, systems, mistakes, customer/business impact, policy, and success. Management was also asked about urgency, authority, and budget.

## Manager responses
- A request arrives at the front desk; staff check eligibility, obtain approval when needed, update the membership platform, and confirm.
- **Estimate:** about 8 freeze requests/week; no report checked.
- **Estimate:** normal request about 5 minutes.
- Normal requests belong to front-desk staff; exceptions go to the membership manager.
- Overdue balances and medical-freeze requests can require manager review.
- Staff review is intentional: eligibility depends on membership type.
- Incorrect entry can require record correction and an explanation of an unexpected charge; frequency is unknown.
- Interruptions are frustrating, but impact is unquantified and there is no fixed deadline.
- Better means fewer repetitive steps and clearer status while retaining eligibility review.
- The owner can approve bounded analysis. Budget is unknown and implementation is not approved.

## Employee responses
- **Estimate:** closer to 3–4 freeze requests most weeks, with seasonal spikes.
- Staff read email/take a call, find the account, check notes, seek help if eligibility is unclear, update the platform, note a spreadsheet, and reply.
- **Estimate:** simple requests may take 5 minutes; an overdue-balance exception may take 15–20. Nothing has been timed.
- Overdue balances or missing membership notes interrupt the normal path.
- Some members ask why approval is required; complaint and abandonment volume is unknown.

## Known facts in this scenario
These are confirmed only as consistent fictional participant statements, not measured Local Works/customer results:
- Staff review implements a stated eligibility policy.
- The described work touches multiple systems and roles.
- Exceptions receive different handling from routine requests.

## Estimates
- Manager: about 8 freeze requests/week and 5 minutes for a normal request.
- Employee: about 3–4/week; about 5 minutes normally and 15–20 for one exception type.

## Contradictions
Weekly frequency estimates conflict. They are preserved separately; they are not averaged. Representative volume requires validation. The time accounts are compatible for simple work but reveal that the manager's normal-case estimate does not describe exceptions.

## Unknowns
Measured volume, seasonality, average/total handling time, correction rate, complaint/abandonment rate, financial effect, platform capabilities, integration behavior, access constraints, and available budget.

## Revised problem understanding
**Before discovery:** “Members cannot self-manage account changes.”

**After discovery:** Certain membership freezes require policy-dependent staff review, and staff appear to perform several manual administrative steps across systems. Frequency, total handling burden, errors, and customer impact remain unmeasured.

This does not imply removing staff or building a portal/app. The apparent friction changed from a presumed lack of self-service to a policy-dependent workflow whose burden still needs validation.
