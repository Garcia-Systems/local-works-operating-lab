# Harbor Fitness Customer-Fit Hypothesis

> Harbor Fitness is a fictional two-location gym. This assessment is an **initial hypothesis—not validated customer evidence**.

## What makes it potentially interesting

- Memberships and customer interactions recur.
- Membership and account-management workflows involve both members and staff.
- Some staff involvement and a visible workflow concern exist.
- Harbor Fitness already uses membership software, which gives the workflow a real operating context while also creating constraints.
- Local management appears reachable.

These signals suggest a workflow worth examining. They do not establish severity, economics, feasibility, or a purchase.

## What remains unknown

- Actual workflow frequency and volume
- Staff time consumed
- Actual member complaints and the number of affected members
- Business impact, including delay, errors, retention, or revenue effects
- Management urgency and willingness to act
- Who has purchasing authority and which stakeholders must approve
- Budget and qualitative economic capacity
- Existing membership-software capabilities and configuration options
- Contract, vendor, data-access, security, or integration restrictions
- Technical and delivery feasibility
- A baseline and a useful way to measure improvement
- Whether any repeat opportunity exists after a responsible first engagement

Each item is an unknown—not negative evidence. In particular, “budget unknown” does not mean “no budget,” and “authority unknown” does not mean “no authority.”

## Current conclusion

**Current fit: PROMISING / REQUIRES VALIDATION.**

Harbor Fitness appears sufficiently interesting to justify a Digital Friction Audit. The audit should establish whether the concern represents meaningful friction and whether further discovery is proportionate. This is **not** equivalent to saying Harbor Fitness should buy a Local Works project, needs custom software, or belongs to a proven Local Works vertical.
