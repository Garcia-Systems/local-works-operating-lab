# Harbor Fitness — Opportunity Assessment

> Harbor Fitness is fictional. This teaching assessment uses Chapter 6 evidence;
> it is not a Local Works customer result.

**Business:** Harbor Fitness (fictional)  
**Source audit:** `06-digital-friction-audit.md`  
**Opportunity candidate:** Membership Account Management  
**Workflow:** Creating and making routine changes to membership accounts

## Source findings and grouping

1. **Routine membership changes.** Public instructions say freezes and
   cancellations require front-desk contact. The audit treats inconvenience and
   staff burden as hypotheses, not observed results.
2. **Clean join form, hidden re-entry.** A fictional employee demonstration and
   statement corroborate that staff copy completed online join forms into a separate
   membership system.

These findings may belong together because both concern getting membership-account
information into, or changing it within, the operational membership workflow. That
relationship remains a framing hypothesis: discovery must determine whether the
same people, systems, handoffs, and constraints are actually involved. “Membership
comparison” is not grouped; it concerns prospect understanding before account
management, and Chapter 6 did not establish a shared process.

## Current problem statement

Routine membership-account creation and changes **appear** to require staff
intervention and, in at least the demonstrated join path, entry into a separate
system. This may create member inconvenience and repetitive administrative work.

## Known

- Public instructions require front-desk contact for freezes and cancellations.
- The demonstrated join workflow includes staff re-entry into a separate membership
  system.
- The findings identify customer, employee, and manager interests.
- Chapter 6 describes re-entry as frequent and its evidence as corroborated, but no
  measured operational dataset is available.

## Unknown

- Freeze and cancellation request volumes, handling time, exceptions, and member impact.
- Join volume, re-entry time, error rate, correction work, and downstream impact.
- Whether both findings truly share systems, staff steps, or a root condition.
- Billing implications, contract or policy constraints, retention value, and risks.
- Existing software capabilities and limits; no technical feasibility is established.
- Sponsor authority, urgency, budget, and willingness to support investigation.
- A baseline and practical outcome measures.

Unknown frequency is not low frequency; unknown budget is not no budget; unknown
technical feasibility is not impossibility.

## Signals

**Positive signals**
- Manual join-data re-entry is corroborated by fictional internal evidence.
- Routine-change instructions visibly require staff contact.
- The possible workflow crosses multiple affected parties and matters to service,
  retention, customer experience, and staff productivity.
- Frequency, handling time, errors, and contacts appear measurable in principle.

**Negative signals**
- The routine-change burden itself has not been observed or measured.
- A retention conversation may be intentional value rather than waste.
- The apparent grouping may prove to contain independent processes.

**Hard disqualifiers:** None identified in the fictional evidence.

## Gate judgment

**Problem potential: STRONG (provisional).** There is corroborated internal re-entry
plus a related, visible staff-contact path, but magnitude and common cause remain
unmeasured.

**Commercial fit: MODERATE / INCOMPLETE.** The case offers plausible access for a
bounded investigation, but authority, urgency, willingness, and economic fit are
not established.

**Economic plausibility: MODERATE / UNKNOWN MAGNITUDE.** Repetition could conceivably
justify professional investigation. This is only a reason to measure—not quantified
problem economics.

**Current decision: DISCOVERY_WARRANTED.** Focused discovery is proportionate because
one manual workflow is corroborated and the routine-change path may be related and
material. Discovery must test that grouping rather than assume it.

This decision means **the problem appears worth investigating**. It does not mean
Harbor Fitness should purchase a project, that a member portal should be built, that
custom software is justified, or that any implementation is approved.

## Next questions and action

- Map join, freeze, and cancellation steps from request through account and billing changes.
- Which steps, systems, owners, exceptions, and policy reasons are shared?
- What are volumes, handling times, errors, repeat contacts, and member consequences?
- Who can sponsor investigation, and what change—if any—matters now?
- What baseline could show whether a later improvement helped?

**Next action:** Conduct bounded discovery to establish workflow boundaries, burden,
importance, authority, and measurability. Select no solution yet.
