# Harbor Fitness: Initial Request

> **Fictional running case:** Harbor Fitness is invented for this operating lab. Nothing here reports a real customer result.

## Customer statement

> “We think we need a new member portal.”

## Known facts

- Harbor Fitness is a two-location independent gym.
- It has recurring memberships.
- Staff handles membership questions.
- Customers can find information online.
- Some membership changes require staff contact.
- Billing is handled through an existing membership system.
- Management suspects the joining and account-management experience is frustrating.

These facts establish context, not proof of the cause, scale, or value of any friction.

## Unknowns

- What friction are members and staff actually experiencing?
- Which joining and account-management workflows are involved?
- Where, when, and for whom does each workflow fail or slow down?
- How often does the suspected friction occur?
- What burden or business effect does it create?
- Which membership changes require contact, and why?
- What can the current membership system already configure or support?
- Are policy, process, training, integration, or technical constraints involved?
- Who has authority to change the workflow, and how urgent is it?
- Would addressing the validated problem be economically sensible?

## Premature assumption

A new member portal is required. “Portal” is management's requested solution language, not a validated problem or an approved technical response.

## Next Local Works service

**Digital Friction Audit.** The proper first question is: **What friction are members and staff actually experiencing?** The audit may recommend discovery, identify a simpler response, find an issue not worth pursuing, or find no meaningful issue. It does not guarantee a portal or any implementation.
