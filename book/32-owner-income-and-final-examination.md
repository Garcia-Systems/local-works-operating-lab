# Chapter 32 — Owner Income and the Final Local Works Examination

> **IN PROGRESS after 32A.** This part builds one fictional baseline engine. It
> does not calculate owner income, run scenarios or Monte Carlo, or issue the
> final business verdict.

## 1. The final question

Can the whole Local Works operating system eventually produce stable,
attractive owner income after demand, conversion, delivery, support, incidents,
cash timing, concentration, dependencies, and finite owner capacity? Part A asks
the prerequisite question: can those parts behave coherently for 36 months?

## 2. Why one project proves nothing

A profitable project can coexist with a sales drought, an accumulating support
tail, a late receivable, or an exhausted owner. A project result is not a
multi-year operating result.

## 3. The complete Local Works lifecycle

The simulation joins prospect, lead, qualification, discovery, proposal,
signature, queue, aggregate delivery, stabilization, support or quiet health,
expansion, referral, risk, churn, and closure. It reuses Chapter 31's capacity,
cash, contribution, pipeline, start-gate, and concentration concepts.

## 4. What the capstone can and cannot prove

It can expose whether stated assumptions produce internally coherent monthly
flows and adverse states. Fictional arithmetic cannot prove demand, conversion,
prices, performance, resilience, owner income, or business viability.

## 5. Assumptions vs evidence

Every major input carries `HYPOTHESIS`, `SIMULATION_ASSUMPTION`, `OBSERVED`,
`MEASURED`, `DERIVED`, or `UNKNOWN`. Most baseline inputs are simulation
assumptions. Repeated model use never upgrades evidence.

## 6. The 36-month simulation

Thirty-six monthly snapshots and three annual summaries keep pipeline, revenue,
contribution, and cash separate. The seeded baseline is repeatable, not random
evidence and not a Monte Carlo experiment.

## 7. Startup period

Month one has no customers, a partial lead ramp, and no instant close. Maturity
must emerge through the lifecycle rather than being placed in the opening state.

## 8. Acquisition

Outreach and later referrals create leads. Qualification, audit, discovery, and
proposal work consume owner time; constrained capacity limits progress.

## 9. Sales-cycle timing

A proposal cannot close before the configured two-month minimum age. Deferred
opportunities remain pipeline—not revenue.

## 10. Solution-path mix

Wins rotate through configure, integrate, automate, and custom-build examples.
Some evaluated proposals become `LEAVE_ALONE` or `NO_DEAL`; software is not the
automatic destination.

## 11. Project starts

Signature is not authorization. Chapter 31-style gates preserve owner, partner,
support, incident, concurrency, reserve, and cash constraints. Unsafe starts queue.

## 12. Delivery

Started projects advance through kickoff, requirements, implementation, QA,
acceptance, launch, and closeout at aggregate level. Customer decisions, partner
delay, change, rework, or vendor dependency are represented by a bounded delay.

## 13. Support tail

Completed work may adopt no support, pay-as-you-go, light, managed, or vendor-only
support. Routine paid work accumulates while warranty and goodwill remain visible.

## 14. Incidents

Aggregate operational incidents consume owner hours and partner cost. A shared
fictional vendor can create one correlated incident; no cyberattack is simulated.

## 15. Expansion

A stable relationship may emit a signal. Qualification and proposal effort still
stand between that signal and a separately priced expansion sale.

## 16. Referrals

Stable customers may create referral leads. A referral is only a lead and receives
no automatic qualification or close.

## 17. Churn

Support can continue, go quiet, or churn. A complete project with no continuing
support may remain quiet and healthy, so lack of activity is not automatically bad.

## 18. Portfolio state

Each month records funnel stages, signed/queued/active/completed projects,
supported and quiet customers, incidents, expansion, churn, risks, and decisions.

## 19. Revenue

Project, support, and expansion revenue are explicit. Neither lead volume nor
proposal value appears as revenue.

## 20. Contribution

Contribution subtracts delivery, other direct, support-partner, warranty,
goodwill, and incident burden. It is not revenue and it is not cash.

## 21. Cash

Deposits, delayed final receipts, early partner payments, overhead, commitments,
and a protected reserve create an intramonth cash view. Negative cash raises a
working-capital failure instead of assuming free financing.

## 22. Owner workload

Sales, audit, discovery, proposal, design, coordination, QA, support, incidents,
relationships, and administration all draw from sustainable and temporary limits.
Overload causes deferral, queue growth, or backlog—not invisible extra labor.

## 23. Pipeline behavior

Pipeline health ranges from weak to excessive for capacity. Delivery can reduce
sales work now and produce a later cliff; excess pipeline can itself be unhealthy.

## 24. Concentration

Largest customer shares differ for revenue, contribution, hours, and support.
Primary-partner and shared-vendor dependence are recorded separately.

## 25. Owner absence

Month 20 removes a planned week (32 hours). The model does not make the owner work
during leave, and start/progression decisions see the reduced capacity.

## 26. Baseline scenario

The sole Part A scenario uses modest startup demand, gradual sales, bounded
support, occasional incidents and expansion/referrals, delayed cash, finite
capacity, and one absence. It intentionally exposes a queue and cash stress.

## 27. Year 1

The fictional startup produces 54 leads, 10 sales, $144,623 revenue, $58,623
contribution, and a -$11,630 minimum cash position. No project completes yet
because start gates and the initial cash reserve constrain delivery.

## 28. Year 2

The fictional second year produces 63 leads, 9 sales, 4 completions, $153,838
revenue, and $71,768 contribution. Support, expansion, referral, and churn begin.

## 29. Year 3

The fictional third year produces 61 leads, 11 sales, 4 completions, $186,235
revenue, and $83,028 contribution. Support and incident burden are now more visible.

## 30. Baseline bottleneck

Cash is the present baseline constraint because the modeled intramonth minimum is
negative. Partner fragility and a 21-project ending queue are major warnings too.
This is a bottleneck finding, not a final business verdict.

## 31. What remains for the final examination

32B still owns scenario comparison, owner-income and break-even work, sensitivity,
and Monte Carlo. 32C still owns the final verdict, real-world validation plan, and
production-application requirements. No final happy ending is asserted in 32A.
