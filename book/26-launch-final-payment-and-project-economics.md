# Chapter 26 — Launch, Final Payment, and Project Economics

> **FICTIONAL TRAINING CHAPTER.** No real system is deployed, no payment is collected, and no customer result is claimed.

A project is not successful merely because software works. Delivery truth, commercial truth, and economic truth must each be established.

## 1. Accepted is not launched

Acceptance establishes that agreed criteria passed in the reviewed environment. Production remains a separately authorized state: ACCEPTED → LAUNCH_PENDING → LAUNCH_READY → LAUNCHED → STABILIZING → COMMERCIAL_CLOSEOUT → CLOSED. Access, timing, reversal, communication, and vendor coordination can still prevent launch.

## 2. Launch readiness

Check acceptance, blocking defects, known-issue disclosure, production access, proportional configuration/deployment steps, available people, recovery/reversal, customer communication, verification, escalation contacts, dependencies, and commercial prerequisites. A nonblocking known issue can yield READY_WITH_MONITORED_RISKS; a blocking defect cannot.

## 3. Launch by solution type

Configure means enable settings; integrate means activate a connection; automate means enable a workflow; custom build may require deployment. Match controls to the solution. Harbor is configuration-first, not fictional custom infrastructure.

## 4. Cutover

Select immediate, scheduled, phased, pilot, parallel, feature-toggle, manual transition, or not applicable according to business risk. Complexity is not maturity, and nights/weekends are not automatically safer.

## 5. Rollback and reversal

Record whether reversal is available, partial, manual, unavailable, not applicable, or unknown. Preserve old configuration or prior process where relevant. Data integrity, access/security, unusable core workflow, repeated wrong processing, and severe impact deserve pause or rollback consideration.

## 6. Launch authority

Name the customer, Local Works, partner, vendor, or shared party who authorizes, performs, verifies, communicates, and decides rollback. Production authority cannot be implied.

## 7. Launch communication

Before launch record what changes, when, expected impact, and contact; afterwards record completion, known issues, watch points, and support path. The lab does not send messages.

## 8. Production verification

Immediately sample critical workflow, state/data, notification, permissions, dependency, duplicate/error, and staff steps. This independent production result can fail after staging acceptance and is not full QA.

## 9. Stabilization

A short window observes conditions that appear only after activation. Record STABLE, MONITORING, ISSUES_FOUND, ROLLBACK_REQUIRED, or EXTENDED_STABILIZATION; do not quietly begin unlimited support.

## 10. Launch incidents

Record severity, impact, evidence, response, owner, communication, and recheck. Choose continue, continue-with-fix, pause, rollback, partial disablement, or vendor escalation from context—not a magic threshold.

## 11. Commercial completion

Delivery completion differs from commercial completion. Reconcile acceptance, approved charges, expenses/credits, documentation, final obligation, and disputes before celebrating.

## 12. Payment status

NOT_DUE, DUE, simulated invoiced/received, partial, late, disputed, waived, and written-off states preserve collection truth without moving money.

## 13. Final payment

Total charges equal original price plus approved paid changes less credits. Final due equals total charges less payments received. Resolve the fictional cancellation dispute against Chapter 24's baseline: excluded and deferred, not guessed into scope.

## 14. Late payment

Track invoice/due/receipt dates, days outstanding, partner cash already paid, owner effort, and receivable. This is cash-risk visibility, not collections or legal advice.

## 15. Actual project economics

Reconcile customer charges, delivery-partner actual cost, other direct cost, owner hours/value, contribution, cash, and receivable. Actuals can invalidate an attractive pricing hypothesis.

## 16. Contribution

Contribution = charges − partner cost − other direct costs. Contribution after owner-time value subtracts imputed owner time once. Neither is automatically accounting profit.

## 17. Owner contribution per hour

Contribution divided by all owner hours shows business yield per scarce founder hour. It is not wage: overhead, taxes, opportunity effects, timing, and accounting treatment differ.

## 18. Owner time by activity

Separate acquisition, audit, discovery, design, proposal, closing, coordination, translation, project management, QA, change control, communication, and closeout to reveal the actual operating burden.

## 19. Presales economics

Show delivery-through-close and full acquisition-through-close views. Unbilled presales does not disappear, while direct cost must not also include the same owner value.

## 20. Estimate vs actual

Never rewrite history. Keep the original baseline, revised forecast, and actual cost/effort/schedule so variance can improve the next estimate.

## 21. Change and rework impact

Only approved paid changes increase customer charges. Absorbed work, technical discovery, defect correction, retest, and escapes can harm internal economics without becoming customer charges.

## 22. Cash flow vs margin

A positive contribution can coexist with negative cash when partner payments precede customer receipts. GOOD MARGIN DOES NOT GUARANTEE SAFE CASH FLOW.

## 23. Maximum cash exposure

Order actual simulated receipts and payments, maintain the cumulative cash balance, and report the largest amount below zero. This is timing exposure, not contribution.

## 24. Customer economics

Expected customer value remains a hypothesis at launch. Do not say Harbor was saved a dollar amount. Preserve the prior baseline and mark measured value not yet established.

## 25. Value realization

Select relevant interventions, minutes, contacts, exceptions, processing, or rework metrics. Capture baseline and plan 30/60/90-day comparisons without retroactively improving the baseline or implementing analytics.

## 26. Soft benefits

Convenience, lower frustration, clearer workflow, and reduced uncertainty matter. Preserve them qualitatively when evidence does not justify money.

## 27. Project success dimensions

Rate scope, quality, schedule, acceptance, collection, contribution, owner time, value plausibility, partner, continuity, and relationship independently as strong, acceptable, mixed, weak, or unknown. No single score substitutes for judgment.

## 28. Postmortem

Without blame, ask what was estimated correctly, underestimated, or reworked; which assumptions and wording helped/hurt; which partner behavior mattered; where owner time went; and what to repeat or change.

## 29. Partner performance

Keep private evidence on estimate accuracy, communication, quality, documentation, reliability, defect response, handoff, and judgment. It informs future selection, not fake public ratings.

## 30. Reusable assets

Sanitized questions, workflow/requirement patterns, tests, and checklists can recur. Customer data, credentials, proprietary code, and confidential rules cannot automatically recur.

## 31. Case-study readiness

Use NOT_READY, MEASUREMENT_PENDING, CUSTOMER_PERMISSION_REQUIRED, READY_FOR_INTERNAL_SUMMARY, or potentially public. Public work needs permission, measured results, accurate attribution, and confidentiality review—never a fake testimonial.

## 32. Access review

At closeout mark temporary access retained, reduced, revoked, transferred, or reviewed. The record changes no real access.

## 33. Support handoff

Transfer known issues, documentation, contact path, vendor dependencies, warranty assumptions, and operational ownership. Chapter 27 decides warranty versus support versus new work; Chapter 26 creates no ticket or recurring plan.

## 34. Harbor Fitness closeout

Harbor authorizes a staffed-hours configuration activation. The partner activates recorded settings; Local Works and Harbor verify standard/exception routes, confirmation, permission, status, and staff visibility. Old settings support manual reversal. A cosmetic old-copy incident receives CONTINUE_WITH_FIX; a wrong-member update is only a hypothetical rollback trigger.

## 35. Failure: no rollback

Changing production configuration without recording its previous state turns a failed core workflow into avoidable launch risk. Acceptance never excuses missing reversal thinking.

## 36. Failure: done but unpaid

At fictional day 60 the customer accepted but the balance is late and the partner is paid. Technical completion did not create commercial completion.

## 37. Failure: ignore owner time

An $8,000 price less $4,000 direct cost appears to yield $4,000 and 50%. With 100 owner hours, that headline cannot tell whether the deal used founder capacity sensibly.

## 38. Failure: fake ROI

A Friday launch cannot support Monday's claim of 70% less administration without measurement. Label it UNSUPPORTED CUSTOMER RESULT and keep measurement pending.

## 39. Failure: rewrite estimate

Changing an original 20-hour estimate to the 35-hour actual destroys calibration. Preserve 20 original, any revised forecast, and 35 actual.

## 40. Success: healthy closeout

Healthy evidence includes acceptance, proportional launch, verification, stability, payment, fair partner payment, contribution, reasonable owner time, controlled assets/access, handoff, and scheduled value measurement—not optimism.

## 41. Executable exercise

Run `python scripts/run_chapter_26.py`. It creates no deployment or payment; it demonstrates readiness, launch, incident, stabilization, commercial reconciliation, actual economics, cash exposure, learning, and closeout.

## 42. Chapter artifacts

Use the launch-readiness, economics, value-realization, postmortem, and closeout templates; methodology; Harbor record; and production-system discoveries. They remain operating documents rather than product design.

## 43. Readiness checkpoint

The reader can distinguish acceptance/launch and delivery/commercial completion; choose cutover/reversal/authority; verify and stabilize; calculate final due, contribution, margin, owner-adjusted contribution, contribution/hour, variance, and exposure; preserve expected versus measured customer value; learn from partner/postmortem evidence; review reusable assets/case-study/access/handoff; and close cleanly.
