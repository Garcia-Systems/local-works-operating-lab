# Chapter 30 — Expansion, Retention, and Referrals

## 1. What happens after support stabilizes

A stable customer is not an account to maximize. The next sale must come from new customer value, not pressure to keep selling. Review value, health, friction, qualification, economics, and alternatives; then continue, support, expand, refer, or leave alone.

## 2. Retention vs lock-in

Retention is voluntary continuation because the customer receives value, trusts Local Works, and the relationship remains sensible. Withholding credentials, documentation, or vendor access is dependency, not retention. A customer must be able to leave safely.

## 3. Relationship health

Assess customer value, solution reliability, support experience, trust, communication, commercial health, owner burden, dependencies, measured outcomes, future opportunity, and engagement as STRONG, HEALTHY, MIXED, AT_RISK, or UNKNOWN. Explain the qualitative conclusion; never conceal judgment in a score.

## 4. Value realization

Revisit the expected workflow outcome and prior burden/value hypotheses. Record metric, baseline, current value, window, source, confidence, and notes. MEASURED, PARTIALLY_MEASURED, OBSERVED, ANECDOTAL, EXPECTED_ONLY, and UNKNOWN are materially different. Never invent realized ROI.

## 5. Relationship reviews

Ask what works, remains frustrating, repeats in support, changed after incidents, consumes manual time, and changed in systems or priorities. Ask whether an improvement is worth pursuing. This is service and learning—not necessarily a sales call.

## 6. Expansion signals

Repeated questions, deferred changes, workarounds, new processes or locations, vendor limits, explicit requests, staff burden, disconnected workflows, and new bottlenecks can be signals. A signal only earns qualification.

## 7. False expansion signals

A casual idea, one employee's preference, owner enthusiasm, unused budget, Local Works revenue desire, or reusable-looking implementation is insufficient. Customer conversation is not automatically a pipeline.

## 8. Expansion qualification

Reuse Chapter 11: real problem, frequency, burden, priority, authority, capacity/budget, fit, urgency, measurability, feasibility, simpler alternative, and value of discovery. Existing trust does not waive evidence.

## 9. Existing customers can still be bad deals

Familiarity does not ensure clear scope, fit, profitability, manageable support, or adequate price. An incumbent deserves the same economic discipline and the freedom to decline.

## 10. Expansion economics

Separate current burden from recoverable value, implementation/customer cost, owner effort, price, delivery cost, contribution, customer payback, support burden, and risk. No automatic incumbent discount is economically or ethically required.

## 11. Expansion contribution per owner hour

Expansion contribution divided by expansion owner hours exposes review, discovery, and proposal effort. It permits comparison with new-customer acquisition, but is undefined when hours are zero and never makes expansion “free revenue.”

## 12. Support-to-expansion boundary

A ticket should receive support rather than a pitch. Only repeated material friction may become a separately recorded signal. Qualify it; do not absorb changed workflows forever or turn every ticket into a sale.

## 13. Leave-alone decisions

Rare, low-burden problems with acceptable workarounds, high-risk customization, disproportionate cost, or likely vendor resolution may deserve LEAVE_ALONE. Saying “Probably not worth changing right now” is useful advice.

## 14. Retention

Retention includes ongoing support, repeat work, periodic review, occasional pay-as-you-go help, or quiet goodwill with no active work. It is not merely subscription renewal.

## 15. Retention metrics

Counts of active, supported, repeat, retained, churned, renewed, expanded, and referred relationships need denominators, periods, and sample-size context. Cohorts can distinguish acquisition periods, but this chapter does not build analytics or manufacture percentages.

## 16. Churn

Churn occurs when an ongoing relationship ends where continuation was reasonably expected. Record the reason and evidence. Closure, vendor change, internal capability, project completion, price, fit, service, incident, or a new provider have different lessons.

## 17. Healthy churn

If the solution works, staff can operate it, and vendor support suffices, PROJECT_COMPLETE_NO_SUPPORT_NEEDED can be success. Do not manipulate the customer into paying to remain.

## 18. Unhealthy churn

Ignored requests, defects, unclear billing, overselling, communication failures, surprise charges, and unavailable dependencies are avoidable losses. Preserve the operational lesson rather than hiding it in an aggregate rate.

## 19. Churn-risk signals

Use observable evidence: communication changes, complaints, incidents, disputes, weak value evidence, price sensitivity, partner failures, internal teams, vendor replacement, payments, and expectation mismatch. UNKNOWN remains valid; do not infer emotion or treat silence alone as risk.

## 20. Graceful offboarding

Plan documentation handoff, access review, issue summary, data/configuration ownership, partner/vendor contacts, commercial status, and support end date. This chapter represents a plan and does not operate a real transition.

## 21. Referrals

A referral follows real value, stability, support trust, no major dispute, willingness, and appropriate timing. It is voluntary—not payment owed for competent service.

## 22. Referral readiness

READY, POTENTIALLY_READY, WAIT_FOR_MEASUREMENT, WAIT_FOR_STABILITY, NOT_APPROPRIATE, DO_NOT_ASK, and UNKNOWN prevent premature requests. An internal request simulation must remain optional, low pressure, specific, easy to decline, and unsent.

## 23. Referral quality

Ask, when appropriate, about another business with a similar workflow, a trusted owner/operator, a relevant association, or partner introduction—not “anyone with a business.” Relevance respects both parties' time.

## 24. Referral economics

Track referral leads, qualified referrals, discovery, sales, and acquisition effort. Referral acquisition cost may be lower but is neither zero nor guaranteed to convert.

## 25. Case studies

Public proof requires a real customer, explicit permission, accurate measured results, confidentiality review, and honest attribution. Satisfaction does not authorize metrics, logos, or quotes.

## 26. Testimonials

Never fabricate a quote or turn an estimate into a result. Harbor Fitness cannot provide a real testimonial because it is fictional.

## 27. Internal case studies

An internal learning summary can safely capture problem, solution path, economics, delivery and support lessons, failures, and reuse hypotheses without presenting public proof.

## 28. Reusable patterns

Classify patterns such as membership management, scheduling, waivers, intake, vendor coordination, or workflow integration. One observation is a HYPOTHESIS or EARLY_SIGNAL; multiple observations are required for REPEATED_PATTERN or STRONG_EVIDENCE. Do not productize one project prematurely.

## 29. Relationship economics

Cumulative contribution equals project contribution plus support and expansion contribution, less post-closeout incident and other relationship direct costs. Preserve the component records rather than overwriting Chapters 26–29.

## 30. Owner relationship hours

Track acquisition, delivery, support, incidents, reviews, expansion, and referral/admin hours. Contribution before and after an explicit owner-hour value answer different questions.

## 31. Lifetime value—cautiously

This lab lacks a reliable sample for statistical LTV. Report observed or explicitly simulated cumulative contribution; label any future projection as a scenario rather than an infinite forecast.

## 32. Customer value vs Local Works value

Customer value is workflow improvement and avoided burden. Seller value is contribution and sustainable work. Neither substitutes for the other.

## 33. Health vs profitability

A profitable customer may be unhappy or risky. A happy customer may be economically unsustainable. Evaluate both dimensions independently.

## 34. Relationship overhead

Every relationship consumes memory, context, communication, readiness, and administrative attention even when tickets are absent. Hours per customer per month is a useful capacity signal for Part VIII.

## 35. Quiet customers

Low activity may mean the solution works perfectly. Do not invent projects, needless check-ins, or churn risk to create activity.

## 36. Review cadence

Select monthly, quarterly, semiannual, on-demand, or none in proportion to value, risk, complexity, and customer preference. Tiny relationships do not need ceremonial quarterly reviews.

## 37. Expansion pipeline

Separate SIGNAL, QUALIFYING, DISCOVERY, ECONOMICS, PROPOSAL, WON, LOST, DEFERRED, and LEAVE_ALONE. Only qualified/proposed work belongs in an appropriately caveated forecast; an idea is not revenue. Single-customer expansion dependence is a concentration signal, while portfolio analysis waits for Chapter 31.

## 38. Referral pipeline

Simulation may record REQUEST_NOT_APPROPRIATE, READY_TO_ASK, ASKED_SIMULATED, INTRODUCTION_RECEIVED_SIMULATED, QUALIFIED, NOT_QUALIFIED, NO_RESPONSE, or DECLINED. No state sends outreach.

## 39. Harbor Fitness relationship

Harbor is a fictional training scenario. Its launch and incident recovery support a HEALTHY qualitative relationship, but realized handling-time improvement remains EXPECTED_ONLY/measurement pending. Chapter 29 favors bounded pay-as-you-go support. Quiet contact is compatible with success.

Cancellation was deferred/out of scope and repeated hypothetically. It merits DISCOVERY_REQUIRED: frequency, burden, policy, authority, feasibility, alternatives, budget, value, cost, price, payback, and support impact remain incomplete. A custom quarterly dashboard is LEAVE_ALONE because the burden is low and an existing report works.

Referral readiness is WAIT_FOR_MEASUREMENT. Harbor permits only an internal training summary, never a public case. Membership management/workflow integration is an EARLY_SIGNAL, not a proven product. Simulated cumulative contribution is $5,400 over 54 owner hours ($100/hour before owner-time value; $1,350 after a $75/hour value). Expansion contribution per its three qualification hours is $0. Relationship overhead is one hour/month. The final action is SUPPORT_LIGHTLY, conduct a proportional value review, discover cancellation only if qualified, and leave the dashboard alone.

## 40. Failure: upsell everything

A comment that reports could look different triggers an immediate proposal without burden or economics. **Lesson: customer conversation is not automatic sales pipeline.**

## 41. Failure: never sell again

Meaningful repeated manual work is called support for a year. **Lesson: avoiding sales can also hurt customer value.**

## 42. Failure: referral too early

A Friday launch produces a Monday demand for three introductions before value measurement. **Lesson: referrals should be earned.**

## 43. Failure: fake case study

An internal estimate becomes a “65% reduction.” **Lesson: expected value is not measured result.**

## 44. Failure: lock-in retention

Local Works withholds admin credentials, documentation, and vendor access during exit. **Lesson: lock-in is not retention.**

## 45. Failure: retain bad customer

Low revenue, constant incidents, payment disputes, and owner burden survive only because they are called MRR. **Lesson: retention can be economically wrong.**

## 46. Failure: misread quiet customer

A rarely contacting, satisfied customer is treated as disengaged. **Lesson: low activity can mean success.**

## 47. Failure: expansion without economics

Trust wins a $2,000 engagement that costs $2,400 and twenty owner hours. **Lesson: trust does not replace deal economics.**

## 48. Success: earned expansion

After stability, repeated cancellation friction is observed, confirmed, prioritized, compared with simpler choices, separately scoped and economically priced, and voluntarily chosen. A minor dashboard is left alone. A later voluntary referral follows supported value.
